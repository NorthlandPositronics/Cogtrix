## Five Process Groups

PMBOK organises project work into five process groups. The groups are sequenced for any one project but recur iteratively in adaptive (agile / hybrid) projects.

### 1. Initiating

**Purpose:** define and authorise a new project or phase.

**Key activities:**

- Develop the project charter (formal authorisation).
- Identify initial stakeholders.
- Determine sponsor and authority bounds.
- Establish high-level scope and business case.

**Key outputs:** project charter, initial stakeholder register, high-level scope statement.

**Authority typically required:** Sponsor.

### 2. Planning

**Purpose:** establish total scope, refine objectives, and define the actions required to attain those objectives.

**Key activities:**

- Develop the project management plan (integrated plan covering scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, communication, risk, procurement, stakeholder).
- Develop scope statement, WBS, schedule, budget.
- Identify risks and plan responses.
- Define communication, quality, resource, procurement, and stakeholder-engagement strategies.

**Key outputs:** PM plan, WBS, schedule, budget, risk register, stakeholder register, communication plan.

**Authority typically required:** Sponsor approval of the PM plan as a baseline.

### 3. Executing

**Purpose:** complete the work defined in the PM plan to satisfy project requirements.

**Key activities:**

- Direct and manage project work.
- Manage team performance and stakeholder engagement.
- Implement quality and procurement activities.
- Acquire and develop resources.

**Key outputs:** deliverables, work-performance data, change requests, project-team performance, stakeholder engagement.

**Authority typically required:** PM (within standing authority); Sponsor for material changes.

### 4. Monitoring and Controlling

**Purpose:** track, review, and regulate project progress and performance; identify any areas in which changes to the plan are required; initiate the corresponding changes.

**Key activities:**

- Monitor work-performance information against the baseline.
- Control scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, communication, risk, procurement, stakeholder engagement.
- Perform integrated change control.
- Validate scope.

**Key outputs:** work-performance reports, change requests, updates to the PM plan and project documents.

**Authority typically required:** PM; Sponsor for change requests above standing authority.

### 5. Closing

**Purpose:** formally complete or close a project, phase, or contract.

**Key activities:**

- Finalise all activities.
- Hand over deliverables to operations.
- Release resources.
- Capture lessons learned.
- Close contracts.

**Key outputs:** final product / service transition, lessons-learned register, formal acceptance, organisational process assets updated.

**Authority typically required:** Sponsor sign-off on programme closure.

---

## Process-Group Interaction Pattern

Process groups overlap. In adaptive (agile / hybrid) projects:

- Initiating and Closing typically execute once per project or phase.
- Planning, Executing, Monitoring/Controlling iterate continuously across each iteration / sprint / phase.

For Nimbus specifically:

- Initiating: complete (charter approved 2026-04-03).
- Planning: complete for baseline; re-planning happens as change requests are approved.
- Executing: active (Month 4 of 9).
- Monitoring and Controlling: active (monthly status reports, weekly stand-ups, quarterly steering committees).
- Closing: target Month 9 (December 2026).
